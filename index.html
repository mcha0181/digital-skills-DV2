Australia's digital skills divide

Who is being left behind as digital ability improves? A quick look at age, education, geography and online participation.

01 · Skills and people

The 2025 Australian Digital Inclusion Index finds that digital ability is improving, but older adults and people with less formal education remain disadvantaged.

02 · Places and access

Geography matters too. These state comparisons distinguish digital skill scores from access, affordability and online participation.

03 · Three geographic views

Three different map idioms: tile-grid cartogram, proportional-symbol map and choropleth. Symbol locations are approximate; the choropleth uses state boundaries.

What this means for education and work

Digital skills help Australians participate in online learning, job applications and digitally enabled workplaces. These associations do not establish that digital ability alone causes education or employment outcomes.

Sources and interpretation

2025 Australian Digital Inclusion Index · ABS 2021 Census collection methodology · State boundary GeoJSON (rowanhogan/australian-states)

Index scores are on a 0–100 scale. Calculated figures (18–34 years and university education) are reconstructed from published scores and differences. The 2025 Index is based on survey data collected in 2024; 2025 affordability scores are not directly comparable with prior years. The online Census response series is a different indicator and is not a digital skill measure.
